## 正文

Google has released [Gemini 4 Argon](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemini-4-argon-for-robotics/) framing it as a general-purpose physical-reasoning model for robot control, Real2Sim scene construction, multimodal labeling of robot video, and iterative policy improvement, rather than as a single end-effector policy.

In the published prompts, Argon directs a Boston Dynamics Spot through a foot-pedal task after practice in a [MuJoCo](https://mujoco.org/) twin, steers a simpler arm with visual coordinates and HTTP motion primitives, and closes a loop with the Gemini Omni video world model. Real2Sim examples rebuild MuJoCo scenes from teleoperation video; labeling prompts annotate demonstration footage for behavior cloning. Google also reports synthesizing domain-randomized MuJoCo episodes from one RGB photo to fine-tune a vision-language-action policy, citing a rise from 56% to 75% success on the corresponding real setup.

Google reports that Argon scores 87% on the Embodied Reasoning Question Answering [ERQA](https://github.com/embodiedreasoning/ERQA) benchmark using pure reasoning, without helper tools, and says that result leads other frontier models on that test. Argon is available to Google AI Ultra subscribers and through the paid Gemini API, with model id gemini-4-argon in the [Gemini API models docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models). The robotics post uses long, task-specific prompts, coordinate grids, motion primitives, verifiers, so scaffolding is part of the system under test, and Google does not publish per-demo failure rates beyond the single VLA case.

Early practitioner reaction focused on simulation fidelity. [Davin YC Dong](https://x.com/DavinYCD/status/2111387962638471335) commented on X:

> Google's Argon robotics examples tune simulation twins against contact events as well as visuals. That is the part I'd inspect: a scene can look convincing while its objects behave incorrectly. For manipulation, matching what happens at contact matters as much as matching pixels.

Separately, a developer posting as [pulpmatrix](https://x.com/pulpmatrix/status/2110951724508946528) argued for source discipline around Video-to-Sim-to-Real claims circulating the same week:

> Video-to-Sim-to-Real is real, documented in Google's Gemini 4 Argon for Robotics dev blog (MuJoCo twin, 87% ERQA). Cornhole isn't in it: no paper, no repo, no hit rate, and real throws calibrated the robot first.

Specialized vision-language-action systems such as [OpenVLA-class](https://learnopencv.com/vision-language-action-models-lerobot-policy/) policies, [Physical Intelligence's π-family models](https://www.pi.website/), and [NVIDIA GR00T](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/industries/robotics/isaac-groot/) with Isaac-oriented tooling optimize for action prediction under robot embodiments and typically rely on large teleoperation or simulation corpora. Scene-to-sim and generative world tools, World Labs-style reconstructions, NVIDIA DreamDojo-style action video, and Isaac Sim digital twins, emphasize environment synthesis or video prediction without necessarily owning the full control-and-QC loop shown in Google's prompts. Prior Google work such as Gemini Robotics-ER remains a related line for spatial perception and planning. For architecture reviews, the question is whether one frontier model plus heavy prompt harnesses displaces a pipeline of VLA, simulator, and labeler services.
