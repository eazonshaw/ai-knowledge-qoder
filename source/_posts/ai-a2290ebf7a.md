Architecture is often framed as simply making change easier. A far more compelling argument is that evolution is about keeping change safe, clear, and deliberate in the face of constant technological and organizational movement.

Together, these articles offer much more than another collection of architectural patterns - they present evolutionary architecture as a continuous feedback system. Fitness functions signal when a system drifts; comprehension confirms people still grasp what they are modifying; boundaries define where independent change can happen; platforms turn safe practices into repeatable defaults; and organizational design ensures those boundaries actually survive contact with reality.

### The InfoQ Evolutionary Architecture in Practice eMag includes:

*The articles in this eMag were written by participants of the [online InfoQ Certified Architect Program](https://certification.qconferences.com/architecture). They represent the capstone of their work, reflecting the cohort's collective learnings on the intersection of AI and modern software architecture.*

-   **Agentic Fitness Functions: Extending Evolutionary Architecture Beyond Deterministic Rules by Hemant Kumar Mahato, Łukasz Sieczkowski , Vijaysenthilkumar Kuppusamy**  
    This article explores how agentic fitness functions extend evolutionary architecture beyond deterministic rules. It describes a governed AI judgment layer that evaluates semantic drift, boundary fidelity, and architectural risks inside CI/CD workflows, helping software architects, senior developers, and engineering leaders protect system integrity at speed without slowing down delivery.  
     
-   **Comprehension as an Architectural Characteristic: A System That Is Not Understood Cannot Evolve Safely by Jacobus Meintjes, Narayana Rengaswamy, Paul Katsande, Sureshbabu Bikki**  
    As AI commoditizes code output, system comprehension silently decays, creating cognitive debt that threatens safe architectural evolution. This article explores why human understanding must be treated as an essential architectural characteristic, offering actionable strategies, socio-technical metrics, and design checkpoints to preserve intent across modern engineering teams.  
     
-   **Fitness Functions and Team Boundaries: Lessons from Evolving a B2C Platform for B2B by Devora Roth Goldshmidt, Dmitry Kekelidze, Hassan Ejaz, Ifiok Idiang, Sebastian Hans**  
    This article explores how evolutionary architecture keeps systems maintainable using fitness functions and domain alignment. Through a real-world B2C-to-B2B expansion, it covers database schema evolution, backward-compatible API changes across team boundaries, and refactoring service boundaries - offering key insights for senior software developers, software architects, and engineering leaders.  
     
-   **Evolutionary Architecture Across Clouds and Regions: How Platform Guardrails Enable Regional Autonomy by Andrey Morozov, Khooviraj Seewoonauth, Mark-Oliver Masur, Maylin Leal, Swetha Iyengar**  
    This article explores how platform guardrails, fitness functions, and domain boundaries enable evolutionary architecture across multi-cloud and regional environments. Designed for senior software developers, architects, and engineering leaders, it balances regional autonomy with centralized security, compliance, and observability without central lock-in.  
     
-   **Evolving the Vibed Prototype: A Distributed Architecture Path to Production Without a Rewrite by Federico Briano, Michele Torello, Yuri Diamanti**  
    This article explores how engineering leaders can evolve AI-assisted prototypes into distributed systems without a complete rewrite. It describes a strategic, three-phase framework driven by domain boundaries, platform golden paths, and automated governance - enabling teams to slash lead times and cognitive load while balancing rapid innovation with long-term architectural stability.
  
-   **Evolutionary Architecture Through Conway's Law: Federating Ownership Without Losing Coherence by Gururaj Raghavendra, Max Knee, Zahir Mansuri**  
    This article explores how evolutionary architecture and Conway's Law enable engineering leaders to federate system ownership without losing coherence. It describes actionable strategies for shifting from centralized services to autonomous lines of business - leveraging the Inverse Conway Maneuver, zero-trust security, platform engineering, and micro-frontends to achieve true deployment independence.

This collection is particularly useful because none of the concerns exist in isolation. The interesting work happens in the connections between them, where architecture stops being a diagram of today's system and becomes a set of mechanisms that help it keep changing without losing coherence.
