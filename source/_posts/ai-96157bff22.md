## 正文

AWS recently [released Amazon ECS Gateway](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/introducing-amazon-ecs-gateway-a-fully-managed-ingress-solution-for-ecs-services/), an account-level ingress resource that lets platform teams define networking once, and application teams attach services to it with a connector. Each service gets a generated hostname, and neither the announcement nor the documentation describes how to put a custom domain in front of it.

The gateway is described by principal developer advocate Channy Yun as:

> a new account-level resource that simplifies ingress networking for containerized workloads by dynamically provisioning and managing the necessary infrastructure on your behalf, with all resources created directly in your account.

It is available in all commercial regions where ECS runs, at [no additional charge](https://aws.amazon.com/ecs/pricing/) beyond the resources it provisions. The first question practitioners asked was how to put their own domain in front of it.

The split it introduces is organizational: platform teams create a gateway with its infrastructure IAM role, subnets, security groups, TLS certificates, listener settings, and optional WAF web ACLs, then associate it with clusters. Application teams add a gateway connector through [CreateService](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonECS/latest/APIReference/API_CreateService.html) or [UpdateService](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonECS/latest/APIReference/API_UpdateService.html), and for each service ECS provisions an ACM certificate, a hostname, a listener rule, a target group, and a security group that accepts traffic only from the load balancer.

*Amazon ECS Gateway architecture. (Source: [AWS News Blog post](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/introducing-amazon-ecs-gateway-a-fully-managed-ingress-solution-for-ecs-services/))*

[On Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/aws/comments/1xb8o4j/announcing_amazon_ecs_gateway_a_managed_ingress/), commenter zurnout went straight at the hostname:

> I don’t get how this is useful at all since you get a random domain name for your ecs service. How am I going to direct traffic to that? It’s the same thing with ECS Express.

Asked whether a CNAME would solve it, he explained why it would not:

> It creates host-based rules in the load balancer, so it wouldn’t know where to route if you used a CNAME.

Commenter ResponsibleWaltz1479 reached the same conclusion, calling the feature usable "for ephemeral /preview environments if the app is not hostname aware," but not for their existing setup.

[The documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonECS/latest/developerguide/gateway-overview.html) supports the reading: each service "gets a generated hostname in the form https://<unique-id>.ecs.<region>.on.aws," and "Amazon ECS uses host-based routing to direct requests from a shared Application Load Balancer to the appropriate service." Custom domain names, Route 53, and CNAME records go unmentioned. Commenter Kralizek82 raised the one workaround nobody resolved, asking whether a gateway could serve as an internal [CloudFront](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudfront/) origin.

Opinion split on whether the service is substantial. Commenter bioJesusMain called it:

> It’s just ALB and DNS with a wrapper

And commenter aneryx suggested:

> It could even just be a CDK construct on top of ALB/NLB and Route 53

Commenter brokenlabrum answered that from experience:

> Those higher level L3 constructs have often turned out to be a mistake because they need to expose every knob on the L2 constructs they wrap. And if they’re opinionated and don’t, ejecting from an L3 construct without it deleting your resources is hell.

Commenter Aggressive-Height167 put the case for it, noting that letting a cloud team define networking once while application teams deploy against it "seems like a pretty solid improvement." The test, they added, "is whether it actually removes complexity without taking away too much control."

The documentation adds a cost detail the announcement leaves out. A gateway adds load balancers as existing ones fill and removes them only when they empty, but it "does not consolidate load balancers" and "does not move services between load balancers, so several partly filled load balancers can remain after services leave." Each one is billed.

Commenter jregovic noted that more than 25 services means more than one load balancer. An AWS respondent answered that "multiple ALBs are handled by the gateway itself - so it’s not something that you need to worry about." The gateway does handle them, but it does not pack them, so the count still climbs with the number of services.

ECS also "does not repair changes made outside it," so modifying or deleting a managed resource means traffic "can stop," and later deployments, gateway updates, and cleanup operations may fail.

The limits are specific: twenty-five services share one load balancer, against a default maxAlbCount of 10 and a documented ceiling of 200 per gateway, and AWS warns that "if maxAlbCount is reached and no ALB has available capacity, the deployment fails." Listeners serve HTTPS on port 443 only, services must use awsvpc networking in the same VPC and account, and [Express Mode](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonECS/latest/developerguide/express-service-overview.html) services are excluded altogether.

The thread’s most upvoted comment dismissed the launch as "a promotion project," and several commenters asked why AWS Elastic Beanstalk did not already cover the ground, one pointing at its [Cluster Mode release](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/elastic-beanstalk-cluster-eks/) last month. On the other side, dmcnaughton1 said a current ECS migration was early enough to adopt it.
